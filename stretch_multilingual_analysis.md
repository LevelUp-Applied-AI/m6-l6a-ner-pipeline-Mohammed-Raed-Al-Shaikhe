# Multilingual NER Analysis

## Cross-Lingual NER Comparison

This experiment compared multilingual Named Entity Recognition (NER) performance on English and Arabic climate articles using two multilingual models: spaCy xx_ent_wiki_sm and Hugging Face XLM-RoBERTa WikiAnn NER. The English texts produced significantly better results than the Arabic texts overall. Both models detected 93 entities in English articles with an entity density of 7.62 entities per 100 words and a 0.0 no-entity rate. However, the spaCy multilingual model performed poorly on Arabic texts, detecting only 20 entities with a density of 2.15 and failing to detect any entities in 25% of Arabic articles. In contrast, the Hugging Face XLM-RoBERTa model detected 64 Arabic entities with a density of 6.88 and successfully identified entities in every Arabic text.

The results show that Arabic NER is more challenging than English NER. English articles benefit from capitalization and clearer word boundaries, which help models identify proper nouns such as organizations and locations. Arabic does not use capital letters, making entity recognition more difficult. Arabic morphology also increases complexity because prefixes and suffixes are attached directly to words. The spaCy multilingual model produced several incorrect Arabic entity predictions such as "وأكد التقرير" and "وأكد وزير" being classified as PERSON entities, showing difficulty with Arabic entity boundaries. The Hugging Face multilingual transformer model handled Arabic significantly better and correctly identified entities such as "الأردن" as GPE and "البنك الدولي" as ORG.

## Implications for NLP Applications in the MENA Region

These findings are important for bilingual NLP applications in Jordan and the wider MENA region because many professional systems process both Arabic and English documents together. Climate monitoring systems, news analysis pipelines, government platforms, and business intelligence tools frequently analyze multilingual datasets. Poor Arabic NER performance can reduce the accuracy of downstream tasks such as information extraction, document classification, and search systems. The comparison demonstrates that transformer-based multilingual models such as XLM-RoBERTa are more suitable for real-world bilingual NLP systems than lightweight multilingual spaCy pipelines.

The experiment also highlights the importance of evaluating NLP systems separately for each language rather than assuming equal performance across languages. Although the spaCy multilingual model worked reasonably well on English texts, its Arabic performance was significantly weaker and produced more classification errors. In bilingual MENA applications, selecting models with strong Arabic language support is essential for building reliable NLP systems.
